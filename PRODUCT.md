# Product

## Register

product

## Users

MaClaw serves professionals, operators, managers, and builders who use an AI workbench to turn natural-language intent into structured workflows, documents, business actions, knowledge retrieval, and remote or enterprise operations. Users are often in a focused task context: reviewing output, confirming fields, checking provenance, managing digital workers, or supervising agent execution.

## Product Purpose

MaClaw is a general-purpose, self-evolving agent platform for professional work. It combines chat, structured workflow templates, knowledge base, memory, skills, remote sessions, and Agent Dynamic UI so users can move from intent to reviewed deliverables without navigating fixed enterprise forms. Success means the interface feels trustworthy, legible, calm, and precise while keeping human confirmation and auditability visible.

## Brand Personality

Professional, composed, capable. The product should feel like a serious enterprise workbench: quiet enough for long sessions, clear enough for high-stakes review, and polished enough to earn trust from business and technical users.

## Anti-references

Avoid dirty red, pink, purple, brown-orange, and muddy warm accents for file paths, emphasized text, inline code, workflow previews, badges, and normal states. Avoid decorative emoji-heavy UI, noisy color mixing, soft consumer-app whimsy, and any palette that makes professional review surfaces feel cheap or chaotic. Reserve red for real errors, destructive actions, rejected states, and failures.

## Design Principles

1. Make review feel controlled: previews, workflow phases, file paths, code, and emphasis should read as structured evidence, not decoration.
2. Use restrained semantic color: steel blue and blue-gray carry navigation, selection, paths, and emphasis; low-saturation green carries success; red appears only for true danger.
3. Reduce interface noise before adding polish: remove decorative emoji, conflicting accents, and redundant badges from dense panels.
4. Preserve operational trust: errors, approvals, dry runs, provenance, and audit states must stay distinct and readable.
5. Prefer consistency over novelty: shared color, spacing, labels, and state language should make the app feel like one mature product.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for normal text and interactive controls. Do not rely on color alone for state. Keep focus states visible, text legible on tinted surfaces, and motion restrained with reduced-motion support where animations exist.
